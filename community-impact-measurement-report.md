# Measuring and Reporting Community Impact — Y Wal Booking & Membership Platform

*Prepared to support funding application narrative on impact measurement and reporting.*

## 1. Summary

Y Wal already runs its membership, bookings, certifications, sales, and volunteering on a single
custom-built platform. Because every visit, booking, membership, certification, and payment
already passes through this one system, we are in an unusually strong position among small
community projects: most of our impact evidence is a by-product of day-to-day operation rather
than a separate data-collection exercise bolted on afterwards. This report sets out (1) what we
can report on today with no further work, (2) a small number of gaps worth closing before launch
or in the first year of operation, and (3) what a recurring impact report to funders would look
like once those gaps are closed.

## 2. What we can already measure

### Reach and participation
- **Unique individuals served** — every member, adult or child, is a distinct record, so total
  reach (not just bookings) is a straightforward count.
- **Household/family reach** — dependents are linked to a parent/guardian account, and family
  membership plans are flagged as such, so we can report both "individuals" and "households"
  reached — useful for demonstrating reach beyond headline user counts.
- **Age profile** — date of birth is captured for members, enabling reporting by age band (e.g.
  under-16s, young people, adults, older adults) without any new data collection.
- **Geographic reach** — postcode is already captured for members. This can be aggregated to show
  the catchment area the wall is actually serving, and cross-referenced against the Welsh Index of
  Multiple Deprivation (WIMD) to evidence reach into higher-deprivation areas — a common funder
  ask that needs no new data collection, only a reporting step (see §3).
- **New vs. returning participants** — account creation date plus booking history lets us report
  growth over time and repeat engagement, not just a single "total visits" figure.

### Frequency and depth of engagement
- **Attendance, not just booking** — the system distinguishes a *booking* from an *actual
  check-in*, including self-service check-in via a personal door PIN for out-of-hours access.
  This means we can report genuine footfall/usage, not just intent to attend, and can also
  surface no-show rates.
- **Programme mix** — events are categorised by type (recurring sessions, one-off socials,
  courses, competitions) and by certification restriction, so we can report the balance of
  activity — e.g. how much delivery is open community climbing versus structured
  courses/inductions.

### Skills, training, and progression
- **Certifications and inductions delivered** — every induction/certification (e.g. bouldering
  induction, self-access, supervisor, instructor) is tracked from being started, through member
  self-completion of declarations, to formal admin sign-off. This gives a clean, auditable count
  of *qualifications and skills gained through the project* — a strong, concrete impact measure
  for a physical activity / skills-development funding case.
- **Progression pathways** — because certifications are per-person and dated, we can show
  individual and cohort progression over time (e.g. "N members progressed from induction to
  supervisor within 12 months").

### Volunteering and community ownership
- **Named volunteering roles** — sessions can carry staffing requirements (e.g. "at least one
  supervisor"), and members holding the right certification can be recorded as staffing that
  specific session, separate from being an attendee.
- **Public volunteer roles page** — a public page already exists inviting people into named
  volunteer roles, distinct from being a member/participant, evidencing community ownership of
  delivery rather than purely staff- or funder-run activity.

### Financial sustainability and reducing barriers to access
- **Membership uptake and type** — including family memberships, showing how many households buy
  in beyond one-off visits.
- **Drop-in credits** — a credit-ledger system lets a member attend a restricted session for free
  by spending a credit rather than paying, or via an active membership. This is a ready-made
  mechanism for subsidised/concessionary access, and every credit spend is already logged —
  meaning "number of subsidised visits provided" is already a reportable number.
- **Donations** — separately trackable from paid bookings/memberships, by individual donor and
  amount, evidencing community financial buy-in alongside grant funding.
- **Ticket sales and pricing** — including membership-discounted ticket prices, so we can show how
  pricing structures are actually used in practice, not just designed on paper.

## 3. Gaps worth closing

These are things the platform does *not* yet capture, or captures only implicitly, that would
materially strengthen an impact report. They're ordered roughly by effort required.

### Low effort — reporting/configuration only, no new data collection
1. **Postcode → deprivation mapping.** Postcode is already stored; add a periodic report that
   maps members' postcodes against WIMD (or English IMD for any cross-border members) to
   demonstrate reach into disadvantaged communities. This is pure reporting work on data we
   already hold.
2. **Retention/lapsing report.** Membership start/end and renewal data already exists; there's no
   current report turning it into a "% of members retained year-on-year" or "active vs lapsed"
   figure. Worth building before the first annual report is due, since funders often want a
   trend, not just a snapshot.
3. **Unmet demand evidence.** We are in the process of adding a waiting-list status for fully
   booked sessions. Once live, "number of people turned away/waitlisted" becomes a genuinely
   compelling evidence-of-demand metric for a growth or capital funding case — it's often more
   persuasive to a funder than attendance numbers alone.

### Medium effort — small additions to existing processes
4. **"How did you hear about us?" / referral source.** Not currently captured at sign-up. A single
   optional field (general public, school, referral scheme, word of mouth, social media, etc.)
   would let us report which outreach channels and partnerships are actually converting into
   participation — often a specific funder question in its own right.
5. **Audience/partnership categorisation.** The system already has a flexible tagging mechanism
   used for admin-defined labels. This could be extended (with no schema change) to categorise
   bookings or members by audience type — e.g. school group, youth club, disability group,
   community referral, general public — enabling a breakdown of who is actually being reached
   beyond raw headcounts.
6. **Volunteer hours, not just volunteer instances.** We can already say *who* staffed *which*
   session, but not for how long. Recording a check-out time (in addition to the existing
   check-in) for anyone staffing a session would let us report total volunteer hours contributed
   — a standard, high-value metric for demonstrating social value and in-kind community
   contribution alongside cash funding.
7. **Case studies and testimonials.** A free-text note can already be attached to a member's
   record. Deliberately using this — e.g. flagging particularly powerful stories at the point
   they're mentioned by staff or volunteers — would build a searchable bank of qualitative
   evidence to pair with the quantitative figures above, which funders consistently want
   alongside numbers.

### Higher effort — genuinely new data collection
8. **Equality and diversity monitoring.** Beyond age (already captured) and postcode-derived
   deprivation proxy, we do not currently capture gender, disability, or ethnicity. Many funders
   (particularly Welsh Government and National Lottery-linked funds) expect at least voluntary,
   anonymised equality monitoring. Recommend a short, clearly-optional monitoring form at
   sign-up or membership renewal, stored separately from the main contact record and reported
   only in aggregate, with a clear explanation to members of why it's collected and that it's
   optional.
9. **Outcomes / wellbeing feedback.** Attendance tells us people showed up; it doesn't tell us
   what changed for them (confidence, fitness, mental health, social connection). A short,
   infrequent survey (e.g. at 3 and 12 months of membership, or via a simple on-site prompt —
   the reception kiosk we've just built for inductions could double as a lightweight survey
   point) would let us report genuine outcome evidence, not just activity volume. This is
   typically the single most differentiating thing in a strong community-impact case, and the one
   most funders explicitly ask about, so it's worth prioritising despite the extra effort.
10. **Session duration / dwell time.** Self-access door activity currently records that a PIN was
    used to enter, but not when someone left. Adding an exit event (even approximate) would allow
    reporting typical session length and real facility utilisation, not just visit counts.

## 4. What a funder-ready impact report would look like once these are in place

Once the low/medium-effort items above are done, a quarterly or annual impact report generated
largely from the platform could include, with minimal manual compilation:

- Unique individuals and households reached, with age and geographic (deprivation-mapped) profile
- Total sessions delivered, split by programme type, plus attendance and no-show rates
- Certifications/qualifications gained, and progression through them over time
- Volunteer hours contributed and number of active volunteers
- Number of subsidised (credit-funded) visits, memberships sold (including family), and donations
  received, evidencing both community financial buy-in and reduced barriers to access
- Retention rate year-on-year
- Unmet demand (waiting-list volume) as evidence of need for further investment
- A small number of qualitative case studies drawn from the notes/testimonial record
- (Once added) equality-monitoring summary and outcome/wellbeing survey results

This combination — hard usage data the system already produces as a by-product of running the
wall, plus a small number of deliberately added data points — gives a funding application
something stronger than most comparable community projects can offer: a genuinely evidence-based,
low-overhead impact reporting process rather than a manual survey exercise reconstructed after
the fact.
